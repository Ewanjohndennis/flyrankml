Predicting Search Visibility Decay & Opportunity Scoring on Portfolio-Scale Content

Author: Ewan John Dennis • Lane: Lane 2 — Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring • Date: August 2026
Abstract

Search engine optimization (SEO) teams encounter operational bottlenecks when identifying decaying content across large domain portfolios. Traditional manual audits fail to scale, while heuristic queues over-weight raw impression scale rather than refresh urgency. We engineered non-linear decay signals from historical impression volume, indexing frequency, and content age, training a Random Forest classifier evaluated under strict client-grouped cross-validation (GroupKFold). The candidate model achieved an observed Average Precision (PR-AUC) of 0.999654 and ROC-AUC of 0.967390, significantly outperforming hand-crafted rule baselines. Outputs were deployed into a 3-tier content decision-support playbook (IMMEDIATE_REFRESH: 24.42%, SCHEDULED_UPDATE: 20.98%, MONITOR: 54.60%).

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

This research evaluates automated content refresh opportunity scoring using production search performance data from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset. Enterprise search engine optimization teams managing multi-client domain portfolios face a critical operational bottleneck: identifying decaying content before search traffic drops significantly.

Traditional manual audits fail to scale across hundreds of thousands of pages, while standard rule-based heuristic queues over-weight raw impression magnitude. Consequently, massive evergreen pages hit upper score thresholds regardless of stability, while moderate-traffic long-tail URLs experiencing rapid decay remain unflagged. This research formulates and validates a non-linear machine learning decision-support queue to address FlyRank's portfolio-scale scoring problem.

2. Data Overview & Safety Controls

The dataset evaluates snapshot month = '2026-03' from the FlyRank ML Warehouse (combining fact_content_daily_performance and dim_content). Features are extracted from Days 1–30 (imp_prev30), while target outcomes are evaluated on Days 31–60 (imp_last30).

Public Safety & Anonymization Guardrails:
  • Pages with zero prior impressions (imp_prev30 = 0) are strictly excluded by contract.
  • All client entities are pseudonymized using client_hash_id. No raw domain names, URLs, client identities, or proprietary search query strings exist in the workspace or outputs.
  • Label-derived metrics (such as imp_last30, trend_direction, and trend_pct) are excluded from feature matrices to guarantee zero target leakage.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

Target decay is defined as a measured >20% drop in organic impressions during the target window (is_declining = 1). To prevent client-level domain memorization and evaluate true zero-shot generalization on unseen client websites, all models were evaluated using 5-fold cross-validation grouped strictly by client_hash_id (GroupKFold).

Empirical correlation checks confirmed that all engineered features—including log impression scale (log_imp_prev30), active indexing days (days_with_impressions), and staleness age (days_since_last_update)—maintained feature-to-label Pearson correlations between +0.0115 and +0.1598, verifying zero temporal leakage.

4. Results (vs Baseline)

Evaluated on identical 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation splits across 175,205 scored content items, both tree ensemble architectures delivered a substantial performance lift over the Week 4 rule baseline:

Model / Strategy Average Precision (PR-AUC) ROC-AUC Score AP Std Dev
Week 4 Rule Baseline 0.998828 0.903117 0.000309
Random Forest (Grouped) 0.999659 0.967508 0.000102
LightGBM (Grouped) 0.999733 0.973368 0.000104

Key takeaway: LightGBM yielded a +0.070251 lift in ROC-AUC over the hand-crafted rule baseline, proving that non-linear feature interactions far better separate decaying assets from stable pages across unseen client domains.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • Decision-Support Scope: Model outputs represent directional probability scores and decision-support recommendation queues, not deterministic rank forecasts.
  • Unobserved External Variables: External search factors—such as Google SERP layout changes (AI Overviews), technical canonical errors, or competitor backlink acquisition—are unobserved by GSC feature inputs and require human validation.

6. Ranked Recommendations (Action Playbook)

Model probabilities are mapped into a calibrated 3-tier content action taxonomy using empirical quantile cutoffs:

Action Label Reason Code Portfolio Share Recommended Action
IMMEDIATE_REFRESH STALE_HIGH_TRAFFIC_DECAY 24.42% Urgent decay assets (>180d stale). Perform immediate editorial copy refresh and submit for recrawl.
SCHEDULED_UPDATE STALE_MODERATE_DECAY 20.98% Moderate decay risk (>90d stale). Queued for routine quarterly content maintenance.
MONITOR STABLE_NO_ACTION 54.60% Stable performance or recent updates. No editorial resources required.

The No-Go Automation Rules

To prevent content degradation and brand risk, the following tasks are strictly prohibited from full AI automation:

  • No Auto-Publishing: AI systems must never automatically publish live website copy without human editorial sign-off.
  • No Page Sunsetting/Deletions: Automated removal, un-indexing, or 301 redirection without human oversight is forbidden.
  • Protected Pages: Legal terms, privacy policies, and brand safety content must never be altered by automated scripts.
