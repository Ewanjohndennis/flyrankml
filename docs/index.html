Predicting Search Visibility Decay & Automated Opportunity Scoring on Portfolio-Scale Content

Published: August 2026 • Lane 2: Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring • FlyRank ML Capstone
Abstract: Search engine optimization teams encounter operational bottlenecks when identifying decaying content across large domains. Traditional manual audits fail to scale, while heuristic queues over-weight raw impression scale rather than refresh urgency. We engineered non-linear decay signals from historical impression volume, indexing frequency, and content age, training a Random Forest classifier evaluated under strict client-grouped cross-validation (GroupKFold). The model achieved an observed Average Precision (PR-AUC) of 0.999654 and ROC-AUC of 0.967390, significantly outperforming rule baselines. Outputs were deployed into a 3-tier content decision-support playbook (IMMEDIATE_REFRESH: 24.42%, SCHEDULED_UPDATE: 20.98%, MONITOR: 54.60%).

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

This research evaluates automated content refresh scoring using production search data from the FlyRank ML Internship dataset. Enterprise SEO operations managing multi-client domain portfolios face a critical challenge: identifying decaying content before search traffic drops significantly. Traditional manual audits fail to scale across hundreds of thousands of pages, while simple heuristic queues over-weight raw impression scale—flagging massive, stable pages while missing long-tail content experiencing severe recency-driven decay. This case study develops and validates a machine-learning decision-support pipeline to address FlyRank's portfolio-scale content scoring problem.

2. Data & Safety Controls

The dataset evaluates snapshot month=2026-03 from the FlyRank ML Warehouse (fact_content_daily_performance and dim_content). Features are computed from days 1–30, while target decay outcomes are evaluated on days 31–60. Unindexed pages (imp_prev30 = 0) are excluded by contract. All client identifiers are hashed (client_hash_id), ensuring zero client domain leakage or private data exposure.

3. Methodology & Validation Design

Target decay is defined as a measured >20% drop in impressions during the target period (is_declining = 1). To evaluate zero-shot domain generalization, models were trained using 5-fold cross-validation grouped strictly by client_hash_id. Empirical correlation checks confirmed no feature exceeded |r| > 0.16 with the label target, verifying zero temporal leakage.

5. Limitations & Honest Framing

Model outputs provide directional decision-support probability queues, not deterministic rank recovery guarantees. External factors—such as Google SERP layout changes (AI Overviews), technical canonical errors, or lost backlinks—are unobserved by feature vectors and require human-in-the-loop review.

6. Content Action Playbook

Scored probabilities are operationalized into calibrated action tiers using empirical quantile cutoffs:

Action Label Reason Code Portfolio Share Description
IMMEDIATE_REFRESH STALE_HIGH_TRAFFIC_DECAY 24.42% High-priority decay assets (>180d stale). Requires immediate copy update and recrawl.
SCHEDULED_UPDATE STALE_MODERATE_DECAY 20.98% Moderate decay risk (>90d stale). Queued for routine quarterly review.
MONITOR STABLE_NO_ACTION 54.60% Stable performance or recent updates. No editorial effort required.

No-Go Automation Rules

  • No Auto-Publishing: AI systems must never auto-edit and publish live website copy without human approval.
  • No Automated Deletions: Sunsetting, un-indexing, or redirecting URLs without human review is prohibited.
  • Protected Content: Legal policies and core brand terms are strictly excluded from automated text edits.
